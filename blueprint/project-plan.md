## 1. Problem - What problem are we solving?

Asking a French legal question in plain language is easy; getting a reliable
answer is not, for three structural reasons. First, the answer is scattered:
the article you find often refers to another one, sometimes in a different
code, and following those cross-references by hand is tedious. Second, the
answer has a date: legal texts change, and an article found on a forum or
generated by an AI assistant may have been repealed years ago without any
warning. Third, general-purpose AI assistants answer with false confidence:
they conclude where they should only locate, and never say what they didn't
check.

Legirag is an AI agent you ask a legal question to in plain French. It
identifies the relevant code or codes, recursively unfolds the graph of
cross-references between articles (including across different codes), dates
every text it cites, cites the exact article and subdivision, and explicitly
states what it does not cover.

## 2. Users - Who is this for?

- **The general public** - individuals, small business owners, students,
  journalists - anyone with a precise legal question and neither the time nor
  the expertise to navigate official legal codes. They expect an answer in
  plain French, immediate, sourced, and verifiable with one click to the
  official text.
- **Developers and integrators** who want to add reliable legal search to
  their own tools, through the public MCP server the project exposes
  (third-party agents such as Claude Desktop, automation workflows).
- **Advanced users** who want to audit the agent's reasoning (the trace of
  tools it called, the time-travel view of a text's past versions) to verify
  an answer's reliability themselves rather than taking it on faith.

## 3. Features - What does the MVP need?

**Core, non-negotiable:**

- Ask a question in plain French, with no dropdown of codes to pick from
- Automatic routing to the relevant code(s) for the question
- Grounded answers: every claim carries a precise article and subdivision,
  enforced by application code rather than a prompt instruction
- Exact citation of the text, with a link to the official source
- Unfolding of the cross-reference graph needed for a complete answer,
  including references that cross into another code
- A permanent panel listing what the system does not cover for this question
  (local orders, case law, circulars...)
- An inspectable agent trace: which tools were called, with what result, and
  why

**High-impact differentiators:**

- Time-travel view: see the answer as it applied on a given past date
- Explicit flag when an article has been repealed or amended - never cited as
  applicable without saying so
- Explicit abstention when the answer isn't in the corpus, instead of making
  one up
- When the system abstains, it says who to turn to instead (a lawyer, a local
  authority, a labor inspectorate...)
- Every calculation (a deadline, a threshold, a length of service, a statute
  of limitations) is delegated to a deterministic, reproducible tool, never
  estimated by the model
- Detection of a false premise in the question, with a correction instead of
  playing along

**Measured and published reliability.** The project publishes its own quality
metrics (correct routing rate, cross-reference coverage, rate of citing a
repealed article - targeted at zero, correct-abstention rate) instead of just
asserting that the system works. That's what makes trust verifiable rather
than a claim.

**Possible extensions, not blocking for the first version:** a collective
bargaining agreement branch (resolved by agreement ID), a mode for asking
questions about an uploaded document, an automation workflow calling the MCP
server.

## 4. Data - What are we storing?

- **Legal articles** - France's codes in force, with their state (in force /
  amended / repealed), their effective dates, their position in the code's
  hierarchy (part, book, title, chapter, section), and their text
- **Subdivisions** - an article's breakdown into numbered paragraphs and
  clauses, to allow precise citation
- **Cross-references** - the graph of references from one article to
  another, including across codes, with their form (simple reference,
  enumeration, range of articles) and their resolution to the target article
- **Search-indexed segments** - text prefixed with its hierarchical context,
  with a vector representation for meaning-based search and a full-text index
  for keyword search
- **Evaluation question set** - a collection of annotated questions with
  expected answers, used to continuously measure system quality
- **Execution traces** - for every question asked, the detailed steps the
  agent followed (tools called, routing decisions, cost, latency), inspectable
  by a user who wants to audit an answer
- **Collective bargaining agreements**, optional - indexed by agreement ID,
  for labor-law questions that depend on one

Source for legal articles: open data published by the French administration
(Open License 2.0), across all codes currently in force, supplemented on a
subset of everyday codes with a version history to enable the time-travel
view.

## 5. Tech - What stack are we using?

A pnpm monorepo in strict TypeScript, organized into independent packages for
the front end, the API, the agent layer, data access, ingestion, and the tool
server (already scaffolded under `packages/`).

- **Front end**: Next.js, with a DSFR-inspired UI - accessibility rigor and
  institutional component conventions carried over, but a distinct accent
  color and typeface, since legirag is not an official `.gouv.fr` service and
  the DSFR's visual identity (Marianne blue, Marianne typeface, state
  branding) is licensed for government use only; the answer streamed as it's
  produced
- **API**: NestJS - input validation, rejecting any response containing an
  unsourced claim, rate limiting, and a per-request cost cap
- **Agent**: state-graph orchestration (LangGraph.js) with several
  specialized roles (routing, search, drafting, verification), and a
  cross-reference-following loop bounded by explicit stop criteria
- **Tools**: a public MCP server exposing the system's capabilities (search,
  cross-reference following, deterministic calculation, resolving a text's
  version at a given date...) to third-party agents
- **Language models**: served through Amazon Bedrock, selected via
  environment variable (never hardcoded) so the model can change without a
  code change
- **Data**: Postgres with a vector extension and row-level security (RLS), so
  that filtering by state, date, code, or professional branch is enforced in
  the database and can't be bypassed by a cleverly worded question
- **Search**: hybrid, combining keyword search and meaning-based search, with
  a final re-ranking of the most relevant results
- **Observability**: detailed tracing of every request (tools called, cost,
  latency per step), inspectable from the agent-trace screen
- **Evaluation**: a dedicated test harness that replays a set of annotated
  questions and blocks any quality regression before a change ships
- **Infrastructure**: provisioned with Terraform, event-driven ingestion
  (asynchronous processing, automatic reindexing triggered when a legal text
  is updated)

## 6. Monetize - How will this make money?

No monetization is planned initially: the project relies on open data and
aims to provide a public-interest service (making the law understandable)
free of charge. The evaluation question set and the measurement results are
published openly, in the same spirit of openness as the source data.

Funding avenues could be explored later if usage justifies it (higher-volume
access for professionals, partnerships), but none are committed to for the
current version.

## 7. UI/UX - How should this look and feel?

Understated and institutional, DSFR-inspired (accessible, no-nonsense
government-service conventions) without reusing the DSFR's reserved visual
identity, designed to be understood without instructions.

- **Question and answer screen.** A single, centered input field with a few
  clickable example questions to get started. While the agent works, its
  activity is shown in plain language (searching, reading, following a
  cross-reference, verifying) rather than a plain loading indicator. The
  answer unfolds in three blocks: the main rule (one sentence of answer, then
  the exact text cited, with its reference and legal state), the graph of
  supplementary texts needed (a reference into another code is visually
  distinct from an internal one), and the panel of what isn't covered, always
  visible, never collapsed.
- **The time-travel view.** A horizontal timeline you drag a cursor along:
  articles not yet in force disappear, and articles repealed as of that date
  show struck through with their repeal date. Available only for texts whose
  full history is indexed, and says so explicitly when it isn't.
- **The agent trace.** A chronological, technical view: the initial plan,
  every tool call with its duration and result, any failures and what the
  agent did next, routing decisions, and the stop criterion that triggered.
  Reachable from the answer, never forced on the user.

## 8. Deployment - Where and how will this ship?

Deployment spans several targets depending on the component:

- **Front end (Next.js)**: static hosting or Vercel
- **API, tool server, and observability**: containers provisioned by
  Terraform, with dedicated secrets management (never a hardcoded key)
- **Data ingestion**: asynchronous, event-driven processing, with automatic
  reindexing triggered when a legal text is updated
- **Database**: a managed Postgres instance, EU region
- **Key environment variables**: language model identifiers (never
  hardcoded), credentials for cloud infrastructure, the database, the
  embedding service, and the reranking service
- **Guardrails required before any public exposure of the API**: a
  per-request cost cap, a daily cost cap with a circuit breaker, and
  per-IP rate limiting - a public agent with no cost cap is an open-ended
  bill
- **Health check** exposed by the API
- **Continuous integration**: lint, typecheck, and unit tests on every
  contribution; the evaluation suite is wired in as a blocking check against
  any measurable quality regression

This Blueprint's `/release` skill only covers Render or Vercel: it applies to
the front end only. Infrastructure provisioning stays directly driven by
Terraform, outside its scope.
